## Judgment

Carried over from the proposal, unchanged: word-overlap over existing sections instead
of an exact-phrase match or a new structured tag — see the prior revision's reasoning,
still current for checks 2 and 3 and for what counts as a "claim" in check 1.

Check 1's *refusal-vs-notice* design changed twice during delivery, both times by direct
human instruction relayed by the orchestrator, not by this session's own judgment call
(a change against the approved design is recorded here, never silently applied):

1. First relayed note (approval time): a bullet is "met" only when the human edits
   northpole.md; a session may additionally *claim*, in its own record's Acceptance
   verification/Judgment, that its issue satisfies a still-standing bullet. Implemented
   as the original hard refusal.
2. Second relayed note: the human flagged a deadlock — once the issue that actually
   satisfies a bullet is accepted, nothing marks it met on the north pole itself, so
   every later issue keeps refusing until the human manually edits the file. Fix: a
   bullet also counts met once *some landed/done record already on main* claims it
   (`_landed_records_on_main`), checked without a GitHub call by reading
   `docs/issue-*/reports/*.md` off `main` in this checkout. This is the Watch-for item
   "the evidence requirement turning into over-verification" cutting the other way — a
   check that only ever tightens as issues land, with no way to mark done, is exactly
   the kind of one-way ratchet that watch item warns against; Priority #5 (speed of the
   drive) also argued against a check every future issue must work around by hand.
3. Third relayed note (final, overriding the second where they conflict): remove the
   refusal entirely — a hard gate on *order* was "a major problem in the predecessor
   system" per the human directly. Must first stays a **notice**: `otr accept` still
   prints exactly which bullets nothing has met yet, so a human or a later session can
   see the gap, but it never blocks landing. The "met by a landed record" derivation
   from step 2 is kept, so the notice goes quiet on its own as work actually lands —
   this is what makes the notice worth reading months in rather than noise. Checks 2 and
   3 stay refusals, per the same note: they ask for a *recorded reason* item-by-item
   (Priority #4, judgment recorded as applied), not for a global ordering the whole
   drive must satisfy — the distinction the note itself draws.

No alternative to "notice, never a refusal" was live once the third note landed; `none`
beyond what is recorded above for that step.
